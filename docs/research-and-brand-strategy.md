# Manywise — Research and Brand Strategy

**Brand:** Manywise · the 1–2% after-class clubroom
**Tagline:** “Meet us after class. Bring your whole inner world.”
**Founding line:** “Come as nuanced as you are.”
**Strategy date:** 27 October 2026  
**Status:** Greenfield product direction; production-oriented, subject to validation

## 1. Strategic position

**Manywise is a thoughtful social home for people who see INFJ as a useful lens, never a box.** It should help members feel understood without asking them to flatten themselves into a type, perform intimacy, or keep pace with an endless chat stream.

The product promise is not “meet people exactly like you.” It is: **bring your contradictions, context, and changing mind; find people willing to meet them with care.** Personality language can start a conversation, but interests, lived experience, values, boundaries, and behavior should carry it forward.

This document is based on public product and policy desk research, not completed primary research with Manywise users. The needs below are evidence-informed hypotheses. Before broad launch, validate them through moderated interviews, accessibility sessions, prototype tests, and a small community pilot; do not turn interview counts into market-size claims.

## 2. Researched user needs

Adjacent products reveal a recurring trade-off. Fast group chat supports immediacy but can become hard to re-enter; structured forums preserve context but may feel impersonal; public social feeds aid discovery but reward performance; creator platforms provide coherence but preserve a host–audience hierarchy. Manywise should resolve this trade-off around six jobs:

1. **Be recognizable without becoming reducible.** Let a member say “INFJ resonates with me” while foregrounding their own words, interests, seasons of life, and uncertainty. The official Myers-Briggs overview itself says type does not dictate behavior and people can use both sides of preference pairs ([Myers & Briggs Foundation](https://www.myersbriggs.org/my-mbti-personality-type/myers-briggs-overview/)).
2. **Find depth at a humane pace.** Support asynchronous, threaded conversation with summaries, clear room purpose, and graceful re-entry—not an expectation to monitor a live stream. Slack’s durable channels and threads show the organizational value of topic boundaries ([Slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/360017938993-What-is-a-channel)); Geneva shows the appeal of combining chat, forums, events, and access controls ([Geneva](https://www.geneva.com/about)).
3. **Move from recognition to relationship safely.** Members need low-pressure ways to signal resonance, converse repeatedly, and request private contact. Intimacy should be earned through consent, not inferred from a shared type label.
4. **Make thoughtful contributions durable and findable.** Letterboxd turns personal reflection into profiles, lists, and discovery around a shared object ([Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/about/what-is-letterboxd/)); Are.na organizes knowledge through reusable blocks, channels, and connections ([Are.na Help](https://help.are.na/docs)). Manywise can make reflections and resource collections useful beyond the posting moment.
5. **Control exposure and social energy.** Members need pseudonymity, clear audience settings, request-based private messages, muting, blocking, notification quiet hours, and a home view that can end. Privacy choices must be understandable and reversible where safety allows.
6. **Trust the container.** Rules, enforcement, appeals, and moderator powers must be visible. Discord combines platform enforcement, user reports, proactive detection, and local moderation ([Discord Safety](https://discord.com/safety/our-approach-to-content-moderation)); Reddit explicitly treats moderators as accountable stewards of stable communities ([Reddit Moderator Code](https://redditinc.com/policies/moderator-code-of-conduct)).

## 3. Competitor pattern review

| Product | Pattern worth learning from | Gap or caution for Manywise |
|---|---|---|
| [Reddit](https://redditinc.com/) | Topic-first communities, pseudonymous participation, durable threads, community-specific rules | Voting and large public audiences can reward certainty, repetition, and pile-ons; volunteer moderation can carry too much risk and labor |
| [Discord](https://discord.com/servers) | Room structure, roles, live presence, expressive small-group interaction, local moderation | High-velocity chat is difficult to re-enter; server complexity, notification load, and unsolicited contact can undermine calm participation |
| [Geneva](https://www.geneva.com/about) | Multiple room modes, events, questionnaires, and role-based access in one group home | Many modes can fragment attention; live programming can privilege availability and extroverted participation |
| [Circle](https://circle.so/platform/) | Branded spaces, rich profiles, discussions, events, content, access control, and coherent onboarding | The creator/business frame can produce a center–audience hierarchy rather than member-to-member ownership |
| [Are.na](https://support.are.na/help/how-is-are-dot-na-different-from-tumblr) | Slow curation, composable collections, and connection of ideas without feed-first spectacle; current concepts are documented in its [Help center](https://help.are.na/docs) | Curation alone does not create relational continuity, care norms, or an obvious path from artifact to friendship |
| [Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/about/what-is-letterboxd/) | Identity expressed through diary entries, reviews, favorites, tags, follows, and lists around shared cultural objects | Ratings and taste display can become status shorthand; an INFJ community needs more context than a scored object |
| [Substack Notes](https://substack.com/notes) | Lightweight public notes connect longer-form voices and discussion | Writer-led distribution can reproduce broadcasting, follower hierarchy, and personal-brand pressure |
| [Slack](https://slack.com/features/channels) | Named public/private channels, threads, search, and readable history support orientation | Workplace metaphors, unread counts, and inbox-like urgency feel instrumental rather than restorative |

### Opportunity gap

No reviewed pattern alone provides **paced belonging + nuanced self-description + durable collective sense-making + consent-led relationship building**. Manywise should not be another personality meme feed, generic chat server, or expert-led content funnel. Its differentiation is a calm social architecture where:

- profiles contain facets and boundaries, not compatibility scores;
- discovery starts with questions, interests, and context—not popularity;
- conversations can mature into shared collections and recurring small groups;
- private contact is requested, not presumed;
- identity language remains optional, revisable, and non-clinical;
- governance is part of the experience, not hidden backstage.

## 4. Product principles

1. **Lens, never label.** Never infer ability, morality, mental health, compatibility, or destiny from a type. Do not require an official assessment or police whether someone is “really” INFJ.
2. **Specificity over stereotype.** Prompt for “what is true for you?” rather than “what INFJs always do.” Qualify prevalence as a sample-dependent 1–2% estimate, pair it with “rare does not mean ranked,” and never turn rarity into intelligence, moral status, or authenticity.
3. **Depth without forced disclosure.** Rich prompts are optional. Preview audience and persistence before posting. Sensitive profile fields default to private or unset.
4. **Paced by design.** Threads, bookmarks, digests, room summaries, quiet hours, and explicit stopping points outrank infinite scroll, streaks, or urgency cues.
5. **Reciprocity over reach.** Optimize for constructive replies, return conversations, and diverse participation—not impressions, public follower counts, or leaderboards.
6. **Artifacts over ephemera.** Let members turn valuable discussions into stewarded summaries and collections, with contributor attribution and consent.
7. **Consent is a product primitive.** Audience, private-message requests, mentions, invites, exports, and presence each need explicit controls.
8. **Safety is shared, responsibility is not outsourced.** Members shape norms; trained, supported stewards enforce them; the company owns escalations, tooling, and accountability.
9. **Accessible calm is real calm.** Reduced visual noise is not enough. Keyboard access, readable states, plain language, resilient authentication, and reduced motion are launch requirements.

## 5. Initial product and explicit decisions

### First release

- **Welcome:** explain the lens-not-box premise, show the Community Guide, set pronouns/name visibility optionally, choose interests and conversation pace, and configure contact and notification boundaries. Joining never requires a type test.
- **Profile:** free-text “In my own words,” optional “Why INFJ is useful to me,” interests, current questions, contribution preferences, and boundaries. No compatibility percentage.
- **Rooms:** a small, stewarded set organized around enduring needs—Welcome, Inner life, Relationships, Work and purpose, Creativity, Culture, and Type with nuance—plus time-boxed small groups.
- **Threads and reflections:** threaded discussion for questions; longer personal writing as reflections; save and follow without public counts.
- **Collections:** member-curated links, excerpts, and discussion summaries. Attribution is preserved; moving another member’s words into a collection requires consent.
- **Connection:** public replies first; private messages use requests and are off for new accounts until basic trust checks are met. Blocking is immediate and invisible to the blocked account; reporting is available at content, profile, and message level.
- **Home:** finite “Since your last visit,” room updates, saved-thread replies, and optional editorial prompts. No default infinite feed.

### Decisions for launch

- Launch as an **18+ community**; revisit age scope only after dedicated youth-safety design, policy, staffing, and legal review.
- Allow **pseudonyms** and do not expose legal names. Require one accountable account per person through proportionate anti-abuse controls, not public identity verification.
- Use an **invite-wave pilot**, not permanent exclusivity. Publish admission criteria and avoid status signaling around invite access. The “1–2% club” names recognition, not a velvet rope.
- Show no public follower totals, global engagement scores, “top member” ranks, streaks, read receipts, or typing indicators by default.
- Use chronological/contextual ordering inside rooms. Editorial recommendations must be labeled; members can return to chronological order.
- Do not target ads from personality, emotional disclosures, private messages, or inferred mental-health attributes. Do not sell member data.
- Fund initially through transparent membership or patronage experiments only after testing willingness and equitable access; preserve a useful free participation path.

## 6. Trust, safety, privacy, and production controls

### Norms and enforcement

The Community Guide should prohibit harassment, hate, threats, sexual exploitation, doxxing, impersonation, scams, coordinated manipulation, demeaning “type policing,” amateur diagnosis of others, and deterministic claims presented as clinical fact. It should encourage curiosity, disagreement without mind-reading, source labeling, confidentiality, and consent before sharing another member’s story.

Provide report, mute, block, leave, and private-message controls where the interaction occurs. A report flow should preserve relevant evidence, protect the reporter’s identity except where law requires otherwise, acknowledge receipt, and explain outcomes and appeals in plain language. Discord’s user-facing reporting model is a useful baseline ([reporting guidance](https://discord.com/safety/360044103651-reporting-abusive-behavior-to-discord)). Publish severity tiers and response targets only after operations can meet them.

Stewards need scoped permissions, training, two-person review for severe or ambiguous actions, conflict-of-interest recusal, tamper-evident audit history, wellness rotation, escalation paths, and periodic consistency review. Publish aggregate transparency reports once volumes are meaningful; never expose victim details.

Treat self-harm disclosures as care-and-escalation situations, not community content to sensationalize. Show region-appropriate crisis resources, enable urgent reporting, and train staff not to present Manywise or its members as clinical care. Credible imminent threats follow a documented emergency protocol reviewed by counsel and safety specialists.

### Privacy and security by architecture

- Default to least privilege. Every exposed database table must have grants and tested row-level policies; Supabase describes RLS as database-level defense in depth and recommends allow/deny tests per operation ([RLS guide](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/row-level-security)).
- Make realtime rooms private and authorize broadcast, presence, and reads separately. Account for cached permissions and short token lifetimes when access is revoked ([Realtime authorization](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/authorization)). Presence is opt-in and approximate, never a surveillance map.
- Rate-limit sign-up, recovery, search, reactions, invites, private-message requests, reports, uploads, and realtime events. Cap payload, upload, batch, pagination, compute, and third-party spend; these directly address unrestricted resource-consumption risks identified by [OWASP](https://owasp.org/API-Security/editions/2023/en/0xa4-unrestricted-resource-consumption/).
- Separate member content, moderation evidence, and analytics; encrypt in transit and at rest; log privileged access; define retention and deletion schedules; support account export and deletion; minimize telemetry and redact content from routine logs.
- Search only content the requester can already access. PostgreSQL identifies GIN as the preferred full-text index in common cases ([text-search indexes](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/textsearch-indexes.html)); authorization must filter results before snippets or counts leak private material.
- Threat-model scraping, enumeration, ban evasion, brigading, malicious links/files, account takeover, moderator compromise, and inference from sensitive disclosures before beta. Back up and rehearse restoration and incident response.

## 7. Accessibility and inclusive experience

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA at launch**, with the stronger Focus Appearance criterion as an internal standard: visible focus should have sufficient area and at least 3:1 change contrast ([W3C Focus Appearance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance)).

- Full keyboard navigation, skip links, semantic landmarks and headings, logical focus order, no focus traps, and announcements for async updates.
- Text and UI contrast that does not rely on color alone; 200% text zoom and reflow; generous targets; persistent labels; plain-language errors; no timed interactions without control.
- Authentication compatible with password managers and copy/paste, with a non-cognitive alternative to memory or puzzle tests, following [W3C Accessible Authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication).
- Alt text guidance and review; captions and transcripts for hosted media; decorative imagery hidden from assistive technology.
- A visible “reduce motion” preference that honors `prefers-reduced-motion`; no autoplay, flashing, parallax, or motion required to understand state.
- Member-controlled density, font size, theme, digest cadence, and notification quiet hours. Do not market these as fixes for a diagnosis.
- Test with screen readers, keyboard-only and switch-like input, zoom, high contrast, speech input, reduced motion, slow networks, and cognitive walkthroughs; include disabled participants in paid research.

## 8. Brand story and voice

### Story

**Manywise** names a belief: no person is explained by one system, and wisdom becomes more reliable when many perspectives remain distinct. INFJ is the doorway, not the floor plan. The brand invites recognition without sameness and depth without performance.

“**Come as nuanced as you are.**” is both welcome and operating rule. It permits ambivalence, context, boundaries, humor, changing language, and unfinished thought. The community earns the line by making it safe to revise oneself.

### Voice

- **Thoughtful, not solemn:** warm clarity with room for wit; never pseudo-profound.
- **Inviting, not presumptive:** “If this resonates…” rather than “As an INFJ, you…”
- **Specific, not clinical:** describe an interaction or feeling; do not diagnose or assign motives.
- **Grounded, not mystical:** avoid destiny, mind-reading, “empath powers,” rarity, and hierarchy.
- **Calm, not vague:** state rules, privacy, errors, and enforcement directly.
- **Peerful, not guru-led:** “What have you noticed?” rather than “Here is what your type means.”

Example onboarding line: **“INFJ may be one useful word for you. Add the interests, experiences, questions, and boundaries that make it yours.”**

## 9. Visual and motion direction

**Visual idea: layered clarity.** Use overlapping, imperfect forms that retain their edges—many perspectives making a richer whole. The system should feel editorial, human, and quietly social rather than therapeutic, corporate, occult, or productivity-driven.

- **Palette:** warm paper and mist surfaces; deep ink for text; moss/blue-green as the primary action family; plum or clay as restrained accents. Every role needs tested light/dark tokens and non-color state cues. Avoid an all-purple “mystic personality” cliché.
- **Typography:** a characterful, highly legible serif for short editorial display paired with a robust humanist sans for UI and body copy. Optimize reading width, x-height, numerals, punctuation, and language coverage before distinctiveness.
- **Imagery:** member-made artifacts, abstract layers, hand-drawn connective marks, and ordinary places of reflection. Avoid doors, crystal balls, brains, therapy couches, faceless “lonely person” stock imagery, and gendered INFJ stereotypes.
- **Layout:** calm density, visible hierarchy, bounded reading columns, generous but not wasteful space, and clear context labels. Do not hide core actions behind aesthetic minimalism.
- **Motion:** functional transitions generally 120–200 ms, using opacity and small transforms; reserve slower motion for deliberate transitions such as opening a reflection. No engagement confetti, bouncing counters, or ambient drift. Reduced-motion mode should remove nonessential transitions rather than merely shorten them.
- **Sharing:** create restrained, privacy-safe social cards with title, author display preference, room, and brand—not excerpts from limited-audience posts. Next.js supports route metadata and static or generated Open Graph images ([metadata guidance](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/getting-started/metadata-and-og-images)).

## 10. Terminology

| Use | Meaning and rationale | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Member** | A person with agency and responsibility | User as default public-facing noun; follower as status |
| **Room** | A purposeful, bounded area of conversation | Server, workspace, tribe |
| **Thread** | A question or conversation with durable context | Hot take, debate unless it is actually a debate |
| **Reflection** | A longer personal contribution that may evolve | Confession, diagnosis |
| **Collection** | A curated set of resources or consented summaries | Content bucket |
| **Acknowledge** | A lightweight, non-ranked response such as “Thank you” or “This resonates” | Like score, karma |
| **Keep up with** | A private subscription to a member or thread | Follow count, fan |
| **Steward** | A trained community moderator with scoped authority | Guru, type expert, guardian |
| **Community Guide** | Shared norms plus enforceable rules | Fine print |
| **INFJ lens** | Optional self-reflection vocabulary | True INFJ, rarest type, compatibility guarantee |

Use familiar control labels—**Report, Mute, Block, Leave, Privacy, Delete**—instead of branded euphemisms.

## 11. Success signals

Measure whether Manywise creates sustainable mutuality, not compulsion:

- new members who complete boundaries and make one substantive contribution;
- questions receiving constructive, non-duplicative replies across different members;
- reciprocal conversations that continue over time without requiring private contact;
- saved or collaboratively built collections and consented summaries;
- room-level concentration, unanswered-thread rate, and participation distribution;
- report prevalence by surface, time to acknowledgement/resolution, appeal outcomes, repeat harm, and steward workload;
- block/mute and notification changes as product feedback, not “negative engagement” to suppress;
- accessibility task completion and severity of barriers found;
- periodic qualitative checks: “I could be specific,” “I could leave and return,” and “I understood who could see this.”

Set numeric targets only after the pilot establishes a baseline; publish metric definitions and never optimize raw time-on-site.

## 12. Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation / decision |
|---|---|
| Personality essentialism or pseudoscience | Lens-not-box copy throughout; no diagnostic claims, type verification, compatibility engine, or deterministic personalization; invite revision and critique |
| Echo chamber and identity hierarchy | Interest-led cross-room discovery, plural prompts, no rarity framing, structured disagreement norms, and periodic outside-source curation |
| Forced intimacy, parasocial pressure, or predatory contact | Request-based private messages, new-account limits, audience previews, block/report in context, no read receipts by default |
| Quiet design becomes low energy or empty | Scheduled editorial prompts, small recurring groups, stewardship, re-entry summaries, and finite rituals—not gamification |
| Popularity recreates performance | Hide counts, avoid global ranking, diversify recommendations, audit exposure concentration, and cap repeated amplification |
| Moderator inconsistency or burnout | Written decision guides, training, scoped roles, dual review, appeals, audits, rotation, and paid moderation as scale requires |
| Sensitive disclosures leak through search, sharing, analytics, or realtime | Authorization-tested search and channels, safe share cards, data minimization, retention controls, access logs, and adversarial privacy tests |
| “Manywise” conflicts with an existing mark | **Name-screen finding:** searches found no obvious major community product called Manywise, but this is not legal trademark clearance. Before public launch, obtain professional trademark review across relevant jurisdictions/classes and check domains, app stores, entities, and confusingly similar marks |
| INFJ/MBTI language implies endorsement | Prominent independence notice, careful nominative use, no copied assessment content or trade dress, and legal review of marketing and metadata |

## 13. Independence and wellbeing notice

Personality typing is a tool for **self-reflection, not diagnosis**. It does not determine mental health, ability, compatibility, morality, or behavior, and Manywise is not a healthcare or crisis service. Members should not diagnose one another.

## 15. After-class club evolution

A later product direction adds original anime-inspired character art, notebook/club-poster details, and a dedicated **Anime & Character Study** room. This keeps the product’s editorial craft while adding the warmth and immediacy of finding a favorite after-school club. The visual reference is an emotional storytelling grammar—bold outlines, panel framing, expressive color, ensemble casts—not copied characters, franchise marks, or proprietary worlds.

The rarity message is deliberately qualified. The Myers-Briggs Company currently describes people who prefer INFJ as **under 2%** of the general population in one of its global leadership articles, while reported figures vary across samples, countries, instruments, and dates ([source](https://www.themyersbriggs.com/en-us/access-resources/articles/leadership-and-the-intuitionfeeling-nf-process-pair)). Manywise therefore uses **“the 1–2% club*”** as a sourced cultural shorthand with the visible line **“rare does not mean ranked”** and a nearby methodology caveat. It must never imply greater intelligence, moral status, authenticity, or a scientifically fixed universal prevalence.

The optional Clubroom Finder is not a typing assessment. Three situational questions use only the member’s explicit answers to recommend a starting room. Results stay in the browser, are not added to the profile, and can be retaken. This is more useful and safer than a mini “are you really INFJ?” gate because it reduces choice overload without policing identity.
